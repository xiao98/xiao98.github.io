Why an \(L_2\) penalty on gate weights drives redundant directions to zero

Published on 19 November 2026.

Part 3 of 10 in Questions on StructLoRA (ACL 2026).

In Section 3.2 of StructLoRA, the gate vector \(m \in [0, 1]^r\) is motivated through an Information Bottleneck objective, \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{IB}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} + \beta I(\Delta \tilde{W}; X) - \gamma I(\Delta \tilde{W}; Y)\). Because mutual information is intractable directly, Appendix E.2 follows the Variational Information Bottleneck bound of Alemi et al. (2017) with a Gaussian posterior \(q_\phi = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2 I)\) and a zero-mean prior \(r = \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)\) of the same variance. The KL divergence then collapses to a quadratic penalty:

\[\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi \,\|\, r) = \frac{1}{2\sigma^2} \|\mu\|_2^2.\]

Substituting this tractable surrogate yields a regularised objective over the gates:

\[\mathcal{L}(m) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}\big(W_0 + \alpha A \operatorname{diag}(m) B\big) + \lambda \sum_{i=1}^{r} m_i^2, \qquad \lambda > 0.\]

Gradient balance per direction

Differentiating with respect to a single gate scalar \(m_i\) gives

\[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial m_i} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}}{\partial m_i}}_{\text{task benefit}} + \underbrace{2\lambda m_i}_{\text{shrinkage pressure}}.\]

Two regimes immediately emerge under gradient descent \(m_i \leftarrow m_i - \eta \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial m_i}\):

  • Redundant or noisy direction. Changing \(m_i\) barely improves the supervised loss, so \(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}}{\partial m_i} \approx 0\). For any \(m_i > 0\), the shrinkage term \(+2\lambda m_i\) dominates and multiplies \(m_i\) by \((1 - 2\eta\lambda)\) at every step, pulling the gate exponentially toward \(0\).
  • Task-relevant direction. Increasing \(m_i\) produces a strong negative task gradient (\(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}}{\partial m_i} \ll 0\)) that outweighs \(2\lambda m_i\), stabilising \(m_i\) at a strictly positive equilibrium where \(-\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}}{\partial m_i} = 2\lambda m_i\).

Notice one subtlety between \(L_2\) and \(L_1\) regularisation: strictly speaking, \(L_2\) shrinks uninformative gates smoothly toward zero without producing exact zeros in finite steps (unlike \(L_1\) soft-thresholding, as used in SoRA), which is why StructLoRA also mentions Bernoulli priors with Gumbel-Softmax relaxation when hard \(\{0, 1\}\) selection is required.

Takeaway

The Gaussian variational KL bound reduces to a simple quadratic cost \(\lambda \sum_i m_i^2\). Directions whose task gradient cannot offset the shrinkage term \(2\lambda m_i\) decay to zero, concentrating capacity on the directions that move the supervised loss.
