Why input-dependent gates break zero-latency merging, and what that means for Information Bottleneck

Published on 19 November 2026.

Part 4 of 10 in Questions on StructLoRA (ACL 2026).

StructLoRA makes two central claims about its first module: it optimises an Information Bottleneck objective containing the mutual information \(I(\Delta \tilde{W}; X)\), and it preserves LoRA’s zero-latency inference by merging the final update into \(W_0\). Putting these two statements side by side reveals a fundamental tension.

Static vs. dynamic gates across training and inference

First, distinguish updating during training from varying with the input. A static gate vector \(m \in [0, 1]^r\) is updated at every gradient step during training alongside \(A\) and \(B\), but once training ends it is fixed for all inputs \(x\). By linearity of matrix multiplication:

\[y = W_0 x + \alpha A \operatorname{diag}(m) B x = \underbrace{\big(W_0 + \alpha A \operatorname{diag}(m) B\big)}_{W_{\text{merged}}} x.\]

Because \(W_{\text{merged}}\) does not depend on \(x\), \(A\), \(B\), and \(m\) can be folded into \(W_0\) offline and discarded. At inference time, the cost is identical to the base model.

Now consider what happens if the gate actually depends on each input example, \(m = m(x)\):

\[y = W_0 x + \alpha A \operatorname{diag}\big(m(x)\big) B x.\]

Here the effective weight matrix changes from one input \(x_1\) to another \(x_2\). No single merged matrix \(W_{\text{merged}}\) exists: both the gating network \(m(\cdot)\) and the low-rank branch must run on every forward pass at inference time, eliminating zero-latency merging.

The theoretical gap—and the missing baseline

From an engineering perspective, keeping \(m\) static so it merges into \(W_0\) is the right choice. The problem lies in the theoretical framing. In probability theory, mutual information measures statistical dependence between two random variables; for any deterministic parameter \(\Delta \tilde{W}\) that does not vary with the random input \(X\), \(I(\Delta \tilde{W}; X) = 0\) identically. Appendix E.2 (Eq. 12) writes an amortised posterior \(q_\phi(z \mid X) = \mathcal{N}(\mu(X), \sigma^2 I)\) to invoke the variational bound of Alemi et al. (2017), yet the mergeable implementation drops the dependence on \(X\) and reduces the objective to a standard sparsity or \(L_2\) penalty on \(m\).

Once stripped of the Information Bottleneck notation, a static regularised gate between \(A\) and \(B\) closely resembles SoRA (Ding et al., EMNLP 2023), which places an \(L_1\)-regularised gate between LoRA’s two matrices to prune rank-one directions. SoRA is neither cited nor compared in StructLoRA, weakening the paper’s claim to be the first to address unequal direction importance within LoRA.

Takeaway

Zero-latency merging requires an input-independent gate \(m\), under which \(I(\Delta \tilde{W}; X) = 0\). StructLoRA’s practical Stage-1 mechanism is therefore a static sparsity-penalised gate—a sound engineering design, but closer to SoRA than the Information Bottleneck framing suggests.
