Ten questions on StructLoRA (ACL 2026)

Published on 19 November 2026.

Most LoRA variants (QLoRA, VeRA, AdaLoRA, DyLoRA, Sensitivity-LoRA) focus on compression or rank allocation: how many trainable parameters to place in each layer. Not All Directions Matter: Towards Structured and Task-Aware Low-Rank Model Adaptation (Xi Xiao et al., ACL 2026, pp. 2132–2154) asks two different questions: which rank-one directions inside a layer actually help the task, and whether neighbouring layers should coordinate their updates during training.

Rather than writing one long paper summary, I broke my reading notes into ten short, self-contained questions. Each entry isolates one mathematical derivation, architectural choice, or critical check—from outer-product matrix decomposition and Laplacian smoothing to parameter-count verification and cross-table inconsistencies.
