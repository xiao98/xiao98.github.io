Why a 2- or 3-layer GNN hurts performance: quantifying oversmoothing with \((I - \eta L)^T\)

Published on 19 November 2026.

Part 8 of 10 in Questions on StructLoRA (ACL 2026).

In Table 6 of StructLoRA (BoolQ on LLaMA-7B), increasing the depth of the graph coordinator degrades accuracy monotonically: a 1-layer GNN achieves \(81.3\%\), a 2-layer GNN drops to \(80.4\%\), and a 3-layer GNN falls to \(79.7\%\)—nearly back to vanilla LoRA (\(79.1\%\)).

Spectral contraction under repeated smoothing

Iterating the linearised smoothing operator from Part 7 for \(T\) GNN layers gives

\[U^{(T)} = (I - \eta L)^T U^{(0)}.\]

Let \(0 = \lambda_1 < \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_L\) be the eigenvalues of \(L\) on a connected graph. Under the convergence condition \(0 < \eta < 1 / \lambda_L\), the eigenvalues of \((I - \eta L)\) satisfy

\[1 - \eta \lambda_1 = 1, \qquad 0 < 1 - \eta \lambda_k < 1 \quad (k \ge 2).\]

Raising the operator to the \(T\)-th power preserves only the constant eigenvector \(\mathbf{1} \in \operatorname{Null}(L)\) (where all layers hold the exact same update) while contracting every non-constant mode exponentially at rate \((1 - \eta \lambda_k)^T\). That collapse toward a constant depth signal is oversmoothing.

Numerical trace on a three-layer chain

Take the 3-node chain with \(\eta = 0.25\) (\(u_1^+ = 0.75 u_1 + 0.25 u_2\), \(u_2^+ = 0.5 u_2 + 0.25 u_1 + 0.25 u_3\)) starting from a sharply misaligned state \(U^{(0)} = [10, 2, 10]^\top\), whose average across layers is \(7.333\):

0 (vanilla LoRA)\([10.00,\; 2.00,\; 10.00]\)\(128.00\)79.1%
1\([8.00,\; 6.00,\; 8.00]\)\(8.00\)81.3%

3\([7.38,\; 7.25,\; 7.38]\)\(0.03\)79.7%

Notice how quickly the contraction happens: oversmoothing does not wait for a deep 10-layer GNN. One step (\(T = 1\)) removes most of the jagged mismatch (\(\mathcal{E}: 128 \to 8\)) while preserving layer differentiation (\(6\) vs. \(8\)). By \(T = 2\) and \(T = 3\), the layers become nearly indistinguishable, erasing the functional specialisation between lower and upper Transformer blocks.

Takeaway

Because non-constant modes of \((I - \eta L)^T\) decay geometrically with \(T\), oversmoothing sets in immediately after the first message-passing step. Keeping \(T = 1\) with a residual path is therefore a structural necessity, not an arbitrary tuning choice.
