Why keeping the largest-norm LoRA directions loses to learned gating

Published on 19 November 2026.

Part 2 of 10 in Questions on StructLoRA (ACL 2026).

Once \(\Delta W\) is written as a sum of \(r\) rank-one matrices \(\mathbf{a}_j \mathbf{b}_j^\top\), a natural baseline for selecting directions requires no learned gate at all: score each direction by its magnitude and keep the top-\(k\) largest components.

Computing the norm without forming the full matrix

The Frobenius norm of a matrix is the Euclidean norm of all its entries, \(\|M\|_F = \sqrt{\sum_{p,q} M_{pq}^2}\). For a rank-one outer product \(\mathbf{a}_j \mathbf{b}_j^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}\), the double sum factorises cleanly:

\[\|\mathbf{a}_j \mathbf{b}_j^\top\|_F = \sqrt{\sum_{p=1}^{d} \sum_{q=1}^{k} a_{jp}^2 b_{jq}^2} = \sqrt{\left(\sum_{p=1}^{d} a_{jp}^2\right)\left(\sum_{q=1}^{k} b_{jq}^2\right)} = \|\mathbf{a}_j\|_2 \, \|\mathbf{b}_j\|_2.\]

Thus magnitude scoring costs \(O(d + k)\) rather than \(O(dk)\). In StructLoRA (Figure 2), the authors use exactly this score, \(\|\mathbf{a}_j\|_2 \|\mathbf{b}_j\|_2\), as their Top-\(k\) Norm heuristic baseline, matched to the same keep ratio as the learned gate and compared against random masking.

Why magnitude is a weak proxy for relevance

Across all three modalities tested in Figure 2 (language, vision, and multimodal captioning), the empirical ranking is consistent: learned gating > Top-\(k\) Norm > random masking. Magnitude beats random selection by only a modest margin and trails the learned filter clearly.

The mismatch is straightforward. A large Frobenius norm measures how strongly a rank-one term perturbs the pretrained weights, not whether that perturbation reduces generalisation error. Under small datasets or noisy supervision, large-norm directions frequently correspond to sharp, high-variance adjustments that memorise spurious correlations or compensate for poor conditioning, whereas directions that shift task decision boundaries cleanly can have moderate norm. Because Top-\(k\) Norm ignores label sensitivity entirely, it cannot distinguish high-magnitude noise from task signal.

Takeaway

The Frobenius norm of a rank-one LoRA direction simplifies to \(\|\mathbf{a}_j\|_2\|\mathbf{b}_j\|_2\), making magnitude pruning cheap, but update magnitude does not track task usefulness. Selection needs gradient feedback from the task objective.
