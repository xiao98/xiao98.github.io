Why one linearised GNN step is one gradient step of Laplacian smoothing

Published on 19 November 2026.

Part 7 of 10 in Questions on StructLoRA (ACL 2026).

A residual GCN layer updates each node by adding a transformed neighbourhood average to its previous state: \(h_\ell^{(t+1)} = h_\ell^{(t)} + \sigma\big(\sum_{j} \tilde{A}_{\ell j} h_j^{(t)} \Theta^{(t)}\big)\). In Appendix E.4, StructLoRA linearises this step—dropping the nonlinearity \(\sigma\) and replacing the weight matrix \(\Theta^{(t)}\) with a scalar step size—to obtain

\[U^+ = U - \eta (L \otimes I) U.\]

Deriving neighbour averaging from gradient descent

Because the drift energy \(\mathcal{E}(U) = U^\top (L \otimes I) U\) is a smooth convex quadratic, its gradient is \(\nabla \mathcal{E}(U) = 2(L \otimes I)U\), with Lipschitz constant \(L_g = 2\lambda_{\max}(L)\). Therefore \(U^+ = U - \eta(L \otimes I)U\) is literally a gradient-descent step \(U - \frac{\eta}{2}\nabla \mathcal{E}(U)\). By standard smooth convex analysis (Theorem 1 in Appendix E.4), whenever \(0 < \eta < 2 / L_g = 1 / \lambda_{\max}(L)\), the energy strictly decreases unless \((L \otimes I)U = 0\):

\[\mathcal{E}(U^+) \le \mathcal{E}(U) - \eta\big(1 - \eta \lambda_{\max}(L)\big)\|(L \otimes I)U\|_2^2.\]

Writing this out on the three-layer scalar chain from Part 6 makes the equivalence with graph message passing concrete. For \(\mathcal{E} = (u_2 - u_1)^2 + (u_3 - u_2)^2\), differentiating with respect to the middle layer \(u_2\) gives

\[\frac{\partial \mathcal{E}}{\partial u_2} = 4u_2 - 2u_1 - 2u_3.\]

The eigenvalues of the 3-node path Laplacian are \(\{0, 1, 3\}\), so \(\lambda_{\max}(L) = 3\) and Theorem 1 permits any \(0 < \eta < 1/3\) (i.e. gradient step \(\alpha = \eta / 2 < 1/6\)). Choosing \(\eta = 0.25\) (\(\alpha = 0.125\)) yields

\[u_2^+ = u_2 - 0.125\,(4u_2 - 2u_1 - 2u_3) = 0.5\,u_2 + 0.25\,u_1 + 0.25\,u_3.\]

A single step down the gradient of \(\mathcal{E}\) reduces algebraically to keeping half of layer 2’s own update and blending in a quarter from each neighbour—a spectral low-pass filter known as Laplacian smoothing.

Why learned coordination beats static penalties

If smoothing along depth is all that happens, why use a GNN instead of adding \(\lambda \mathcal{E}(U)\) or a cosine penalty directly to the loss? StructLoRA tests both in Table 9 (CIFAR-100 / BoolQ): static cosine regularisation reaches \(82.3 / 79.4\), static Laplacian regularisation reaches \(83.0 / 80.1\), and the learned GNN reaches \(84.1 / 81.3\). Appendix E.5 notes the difference: a static penalty fixes a constant coupling matrix \(L\) across all layers and training steps, whereas a learned GNN induces a data- and stage-dependent effective Laplacian \(L_t\) (including semantic edges between non-adjacent layers).

Takeaway

Gradient descent on the adjacent-layer drift energy produces convex neighbour averaging verbatim. Viewing the residual GNN through this lens explains both why it increases adjacent-layer alignment and why stacking too many GNN layers collapses layer diversity.
