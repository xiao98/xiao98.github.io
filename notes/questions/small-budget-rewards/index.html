Eight questions on rewards built from a few rollouts

Published on 22 November 2026.

Training a language model with reinforcement learning on what another model does with its output needs a reward for every sampled output, and each reward costs many generations. The published setups spend 64 to 128 rollouts on each one. On a single GPU I could afford 8 to 16, and my training reward did not move.

These eight short entries take the reward apart: what the update actually uses, how noisy and how biased that quantity is when it is built from a few rollouts, and how the choice of problems decides whether there is any signal at all. Parts 1 to 7 come from my reproduction of concept-guided exploration (Labiad et al., 2026), and Part 8 from mini-conductor. Each entry isolates one derivation or one check. All measurements come from one training run and small samples, and each entry states its limits.
