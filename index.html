I am a master's student in Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence at Université Paris-Saclay. My research interests lie at the intersection of reinforcement learning, large language models, and autonomous agents. I am particularly interested in how agents can learn from interaction, reason over long horizons, and improve their problem-solving strategies through experience and verifiable feedback.

Before mathematics and AI, I worked as a film director, photographer, and full-stack software engineer. Outside research, I trade cryptocurrency markets with strategies of my own design, which have made me a great deal of money (in backtests).

Alongside my studies, I participate in the Pépite PEIPS entrepreneurship support program.

Selected projects

  • Concept-guided exploration under limited compute A single-GPU re-implementation of concept-guided exploration for LLM reasoning, showing that at low rollout budgets within-group advantages are noisy (0.15 and 0.30 correlation vs 0.78 raw) and max-of-mean rewards favour longer lists. Write-upAnalysis
  • Sequential Gaia2 memory ablation An ablation on Meta's Sequential Gaia2 showing that an agent's useful memory is the files it writes, not its conversation. GitHub
  • Ask-or-act decisions on Gaia2 An experiment on Gaia2's ambiguity split showing that one rewritten instruction rule cuts premature state-changing actions from 15 to 5 and raises passes from 9 to 19 on held-out scenarios. GitHub
  • presidio-fr & FR-PII-Bench On-device GDPR-compliant French PII masking for ChatGPT, Claude, and Mistral Le Chat (0.990 recall, 0.973 precision at 50 ms/doc on CPU), with an open 300-document French benchmark. DocsDataset
  • SmokeAgent Turns French smoke-control briefs into validated NIST FDS simulations via typed specs, deterministic input generation, and RFC 6902 JSON-patch repair (10/10 on FireBench's basic tier). GitHub
  • Fast-Sinkhorn-CUDA A native C++17/CUDA log-domain Sinkhorn solver with warp-level GPU reductions (12× faster than POT and 5.9× faster than GPU PyTorch at n = m = 8192 in 256 MB VRAM). GitHub
