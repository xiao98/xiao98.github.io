Research

Research interests

My research interests lie at the intersection of mathematical optimization, machine learning, and computer systems. I am interested in the computational architecture of intelligent systems: how they learn and reason, allocate limited computational resources, organize memory and knowledge, and improve their own capabilities through experience. My long-term research goal is to develop self-improving intelligent systems.

  • Optimization & Learning Theory. Stochastic optimization, training dynamics, generalization, learning efficiency, credit assignment, and the value of information and training data under resource constraints.
  • Learning & Latent Reasoning. Continuous and latent-space reasoning, adaptive reasoning depth, reasoning compression, exploration, reinforcement learning, LLM post-training, and feedback distillation; how models acquire and transfer reasoning strategies.
  • ML Systems & Adaptive Compute. Compute-efficient training and inference, dynamic model routing, learned computation scheduling, adaptive test-time computation, numerical algorithms, and algorithm–system co-design.
  • Memory Architecture & AI-Native Systems. Episodic, semantic, procedural, and parametric memory; knowledge representation, memory consolidation, executable skills, and agent-native runtimes that coordinate models, tools, verification, and learning.
  • Automated AI Research & Self-Improvement. Automated algorithm discovery, evolutionary program search, experiment selection, autonomous curriculum generation, reliable scientific evaluation, and recursive self-improvement (RSI).

Research questions

  • Memory–computation trade-offs. When should an intelligent system retrieve previous experience, recompute a result, reuse an executable skill, or consolidate knowledge into model parameters?
  • Adaptive reasoning and computation. How can a system learn when to reason in token or latent space, invoke a tool, retrieve memory, delegate to another model, or stop computing?
  • Neural–symbolic memory consolidation. How can experience be transformed into transferable knowledge, reusable programs, and learned representations?
  • Self-improving AI runtimes. Can intelligent systems improve their own computation, memory, verification, and learning mechanisms based on execution histories?
  • Latent memory and reasoning. How can internal representations be reused across tasks, and under what conditions does this improve reasoning efficiency and generalization?

My current work provides experimental starting points for these questions, particularly agent memory and evaluation, resource-efficient learning, and numerical computing. I am interested in mathematics, programming, and scientific simulation as testbeds because results can be evaluated with formal constraints, tests, or numerical solvers.
